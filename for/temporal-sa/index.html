"Ability to provide insightful advice on architectural topics, including application patterns and platform architectures."

Also a retry, because the architecture I'd advise on is the one I got wrong first.

AndrewOS started as a pile of scripts that called each other. It failed the way every hand-rolled orchestrator fails: no way to know what had already run, no way to resume from the middle, and a failure in step four meant rerunning steps one through three and hoping they were idempotent. What fixed it was an append-only trace where every stage records what it received, what it decided, why, and what it handed off, so a finished run replays top to bottom as an audit record instead of a story.

I wrote that and then read your docs and felt slightly ill. It is a shabby hand-built version of your event history. But it means I can draw the failure mode on a whiteboard from memory, because it was mine.

The rest of the pattern set is the same shape: a builder-then-independent-reviewer sequence where no stage reviews its own work, deterministic gates around the steps where a wrong answer is unrecoverable, and a freeze flag that halts all sending when a bounce rate crosses a threshold.
