04 Where I fall short

I have not done this exact job, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

Here is the honest accounting. If the missing column is disqualifying for you, that is a fair call and I would rather you make it now.

What I have not done
  • I have never held the Forward Deployed Engineer title, at GC AI or anywhere else.
  • I have never deployed enterprise software into a Fortune 500 legal department, and I have not sat across from a general counsel or a procurement committee.
  • My customer facing record is advisory rather than enterprise post sales. I worked with individual clients and households, not multi stakeholder rollouts.
  • My formally titled engineering tenure is short. I started at Coder in July, and most of my shipping happened under my own name rather than under a job title.
What I actually have
  • I am an engineer embedded in a business function right now, doing the same kind of work this role describes under a different name.
  • I have built and shipped integrations against other people's systems, including a CRM API integration used by people who do not write code.
  • I know the legal AI problem space because I built a product in it, not because I read about it.
  • I spent four years under FINRA and SEC oversight, so confidentiality, regulated data, and careful claims are habits rather than training slides.

If you need somebody who has already run twenty enterprise deployments, I am not that person and you should hire them. If you are defining this function for the first time and you want an engineer who will sit with the customer, build the thing, and write down what worked so the next deployment is faster, that part I am confident about.

05 How I would start

My first quarter would look like this.

I am guessing at your specifics, so treat this as how I work rather than as a plan I expect to survive contact with your customers.

Phase 01 · Week one

Learn the product by breaking it

I would build something real against the GC AI API before I talk to a customer about it, because I cannot promise an integration I have not attempted myself. I would also read whatever exists of the current deployment history and write down the questions it leaves open.

Phase 02 · Month one

Sit with one customer properly

I would take a single account and follow one workflow end to end, whether that is contract review inside procurement or a compliance check during vendor onboarding. I want to see where the legal team actually loses hours before I propose where the API should sit.

Phase 03 · Quarter one

Turn that deployment into a template

Once one integration is in production I would extract the reusable part, write the reference implementation and the documentation around it, and hand Product and Engineering a specific request rather than a vague complaint about what was hard.

I would like the first conversation.

Your principles say ship today and own it completely. That is how I have had to work, because on three of these products there was nobody else to hand it to. If the missing enterprise deployment history is the sticking point, ask me about it directly and I will give you the same answer I gave in section 04.
