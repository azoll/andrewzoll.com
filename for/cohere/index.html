Why I'd like to do this at Cohere.

The line in your posting that got me was this one: startup-fast, but enterprise-high, with agents that are reliable, observable, safe, and auditable from day one. That's the bar I've been trying to hold all fall.

My best work at Coder was the boring kind. I shipped fail-closed Federal access controls across 26 pull requests, so uncleared users never got Federal history in model input. A reviewer from a different model family caught a Redis cluster-mode bug in review round five that every test had missed. I cut a dashboard's cold load from 5 to 10 seconds down to about 2. None of that demos well, and all of it is what makes a customer trust an agent with sensitive data.

North's security-first posture in finance, healthcare, and telecom is the same problem at a bigger scale. I spent years as a financial advisor under FINRA and SEC oversight, so I know what it's like to sit on the customer's side of that table. I'd love to help a bank or a hospital get an agent from a whiteboard to something their compliance team signs off on.

I also liked your August post on forward deployed engineers building capability, not dependency. When the Coder Agents MCP route on our dogfood deployment started returning 404 after a redeploy, it cost me hours to trace. The fix I'm proudest of was the runbook I wrote afterward so the next person could tell route churn from a real fault. I'd want every customer handoff to leave something like that behind.
